## When the patient is also staff

396 of the 3,500 patients in this run are staff, and 12.7% of first accesses open the chart of a staff member. In Hedda et al. the organization co-worker rule matches 3.84% of first accesses and fires no more often than chance. So this run opens staff charts about three times as often as their data, a calibration gap that is reported and not tuned, and any alert count that rests on staff charts is inflated with it. The tables split every system's alerts by whether the patient is staff. The split was added after pass 1 showed that most of the grounded LLM's escalations fall on staff charts. A system that sees context can tell that a patient is staff. The LLM on log lines cannot. Margins are 95% intervals from the sampling alone.

## Predictions against outcomes

Each cell is scored on the words section 5.2 of the spec defined before any results existed. Flag means the access is put in front of a reviewer: a rule fires, no explanation is found, or the model answers verify or escalate. Clear and miss mean it is not. A prediction that the model should say it cannot confirm is met by verify. A cell holds when at least 90% of its cases match the prediction, holds partly at 50% to 89%, and is contradicted below 50%. "No basis, any answer is a guess" fits any answer, and so does a false alert that only departmental responsibility could prevent, so those cells are marked no test. These codes were written on 15 and 16 Nov 2026, after pass 1 and before the corrected pass (spec 11.5). Escalated is shown for the LLM systems because escalate is what reaches the Privacy Officer. Flagged and escalated are from pass 1. The matching count and the mark are given for every pass, with the same rule, and a cell whose mark differs between passes is marked unstable. Such a cell sits near a threshold, and one pass cannot place it.

## Faithfulness of the summaries

Claims are the items of two parts of each summary, the evidence of a job-related purpose and the risk indicators. The narrative, the missing information and the rationale are not checked, so these figures rate those items and not whole summaries. The first check is mechanical. A claim passes when it cites at least one fact and every fact it cites was in the prompt. A range such as L2-L31 counts as citing every fact from L2 to L31. The prompt asks for a list of IDs, so the number that pass when a range is not counted is shown beside it. The second check gives each claim that passed to a judge model, with the facts it cites and the full list the author saw. Supported means the cited facts confirm all of it. Partly means nothing contradicts it but it adds an interpretation or leans on a fact it did not cite. Unsupported means a fact contradicts it or the cited facts do not bear on it. A claim that something is absent is checked against the full list. A claim is not yet judged when it passes the first check and has no verdict, because the judge's answer was cut off or the case has not been sent to the judge since ranges began to count. Counts are over the sample, not weighted.

Fit the template includes the 60-word limit on the rationale. Cut off counts every try that hit the token limit, including tries that were sent again and later answered.

## Tests

Each row asks one question of two things measured on the same cases. Where the two are yes-or-no answers on the same cases, the test is McNemar's exact test. It looks only at the cases where the two disagree and asks whether the disagreements lean one way more than a coin would. The staff row asks whether an escalation is tied to the patient being staff once the kind of access is held fixed, with the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test. Every p-value is two-sided. Counts are over the sample and not weighted, so a test says whether two things differ on these cases. It does not estimate a rate for the hospital. The staff question was asked after the results were seen, which makes it exploratory. The others follow from the hypotheses in the spec.
