Figures for the whole population are estimates weighted back from that sample, and the margin given is a 95% interval from the sampling alone. It says nothing about how the model would do on a second run. An answer outside the template counts as verify, since someone still has to look at it.

The rules and templates rows are computed on every first access, as in `baseline-results.md`.

## When the patient is also staff

396 of the 3,500 patients in this run are staff, and 12.7% of first accesses open the chart of a staff member. In Hedda et al. the organization co-worker rule matches 3.84% of first accesses and fires no more often than chance. So this run opens staff charts about three times as often as their data, a calibration gap that is reported and not tuned, and any alert count that rests on staff charts is inflated with it. The tables split every system's alerts by whether the patient is staff. The split was added after pass 1 showed that most of the grounded LLM's escalations fall on staff charts. A system that sees context can tell that a patient is staff. The LLM on log lines cannot. Margins are 95% intervals from the sampling alone.

## Faithfulness of the summaries

The first check is mechanical. A claim passes when it cites at least one fact and every fact it cites was in the prompt. A range such as L2-L31 counts as citing every fact from L2 to L31. The prompt asks for a list of IDs, so the number that pass when a range is not counted is shown beside it. The second check gives each claim that passed to a judge model, with the facts it cites and the full list the author saw. Supported means the cited facts confirm all of it. Partly means nothing contradicts it but it adds an interpretation or leans on a fact it did not cite. Unsupported means a fact contradicts it or the cited facts do not bear on it. A claim that something is absent is checked against the full list. A claim is not yet judged when it passes the first check and has no verdict, because the judge's answer was cut off or the case has not been sent to the judge since ranges began to count. Counts are over the sample, not weighted.
